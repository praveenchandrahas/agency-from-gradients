# Core hypothesis: thermodynamic emergence

**Status:** Working hypothesis, version 0.2  
**Role:** Concise statement of the programme's central scientific conjecture

## 1. Hypothesis

The evolution of the Universe creates and sustains gradients in energy availability and quality. In environments where suitable matter, chemistry, boundary conditions and timescales coincide, these gradients can drive the formation and persistence of organized structures.

A subset of such structures may acquire mechanisms that use environmental flows to maintain their organization. Under additional conditions, some may become self-producing or self-replicating, with heritable variation. Once heredity and differential reproduction exist, selection can favour mechanisms that improve access to resources or control over relevant environmental variables. These mechanisms may include sensing, memory, information processing, prediction and action. Intelligence, as a family of increasingly capable information-guided control strategies, is therefore a physically intelligible possible development of the same broad process.

**The conjecture is conditional, not teleological:** the second law does not prescribe life, complexity, intelligence or a direction of evolution. It constrains all physical processes, while the emergence of any particular organization depends on kinetics, chemistry, material constraints, environmental structure, stochastic events and history.

## 2. A compact formulation

> Persistent free-energy gradients can support non-equilibrium organization. Under suitable conditions, some organization can become self-maintaining; some self-maintaining systems may acquire replication and heredity; and selection may then favour information-guided control when its benefits exceed its physical costs.

Each transition is a separate research problem. Evidence for one transition is not evidence for the next.

## 3. Key terms

- **Gradient:** A spatial, temporal or chemical difference that makes a process capable of doing work relative to a specified environment. Examples include temperature, chemical potential, redox potential, proton concentration and photon occupation.
- **Free energy / exergy:** The capacity of a system, relative to a stated environment and constraints, to perform useful work. It is not synonymous with total energy.
- **Dissipative organization:** A spatial, temporal or functional pattern maintained in a driven, non-equilibrium system through ongoing flows and irreversible processes.
- **Self-maintenance:** A system's use of flows and internally coupled processes to repair, replenish or regenerate the organization and constraints required for its continued persistence.
- **Replication:** Production of descendants or copies through a specified physical mechanism. Replication alone does not imply heredity or evolution.
- **Heredity:** Transmission of state or organization from parent to descendant with sufficient fidelity for lineage continuity, while allowing variation.
- **Agency:** An operational capacity to use information about a system or environment to select actions that causally alter subsequent states. Agency must be measured through information, control and intervention, not inferred from labels.
- **Intelligence:** A graded family of capabilities for acquiring and using information to achieve flexible control across tasks or changing environments. This project does not assume a single universal scalar measure.

## 4. Relationship to established science

### Schrödinger: the thermodynamic problem of life

In *What Is Life?* (1944), Schrödinger emphasized that organisms maintain their organization by exchanging matter and energy with their surroundings and exporting entropy. His phrase "feeding on negative entropy" is historically influential, but modern treatment should use explicit free-energy and entropy balances rather than treating negative entropy as a substance.

Schrödinger also emphasized the physical stability and information-bearing role of hereditary structures. This is relevant to the distinction between maintaining an organized individual and transmitting organization across generations.

### England: dissipation-driven adaptation

Jeremy England's work applies modern non-equilibrium statistical mechanics and fluctuation relations to driven systems. Under specified conditions, transitions toward states that absorb and dissipate more work from an external drive can be statistically favoured. Self-replication is one possible route by which driven systems can increase energy absorption and dissipation.

This is a candidate organizing mechanism, not a universal maximum-entropy-production law. It does not establish that all driven matter becomes alive, that all dissipative systems become more complex, or that life and intelligence are inevitable.

### Darwinian evolution

Natural selection explains changes in populations once replication, heredity and variation exist. This programme asks about physical and chemical conditions that may precede those mechanisms, and about the energetic circumstances under which information-guided behaviour can become selectable. It does not replace evolutionary biology.

## 5. Claims explicitly not assumed

The programme does not assume that:

1. Entropy is a force, objective or agent that makes matter seek complexity.
2. The second law requires life, replication, intelligence or agency.
3. All dissipative structures become more organized or more capable.
4. Maximum entropy production is a universal variational principle.
5. Natural selection maximizes total planetary power or entropy production.
6. More energy captured by an organism or population necessarily means more entropy produced by the planet.
7. A simulation initialized with replicators explains abiogenesis.
8. Intelligence is the endpoint or purpose of evolution.

## 6. The central falsifiable challenge

The programme must be prepared to find that persistent gradients produce abundant dissipative structures but rarely, or never under the studied conditions, produce self-maintenance, replication or agency.

It must also be prepared to find that passive or simpler systems outcompete information-processing systems, and that biological organization can increase, decrease or merely redistribute planetary entropy production depending on the boundary and environment.

A theory that cannot accommodate these outcomes is not an adequate scientific formulation.
