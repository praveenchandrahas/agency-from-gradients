# Hypotheses, predictions and null models

**Status:** Initial research register. These are candidate propositions, not established findings.

The goal is to turn the broad research premise into claims that could, in principle, fail. Each hypothesis requires operational definitions and a model or empirical strategy before it can be treated as tested.

## H1 — Persistent gradients create opportunities for non-equilibrium organization

**Claim:** Sustained free-energy gradients can support non-equilibrium structures that would not persist in the absence of the relevant fluxes.

**Predictions to investigate:**
- Some structures persist only within bounded ranges of energy flux, material availability and environmental conditions.
- Removing or reducing the driving gradient changes their persistence or dynamics.
- Different boundary conditions can yield different stable or metastable organizations.

**Nulls and alternatives:** Apparent organization may be a transient fluctuation; may be imposed by initial or boundary conditions; or may follow from ordinary reaction-diffusion or physical self-assembly without a distinct functional role in energy dissipation.

**Tests:** Flux-off controls, perturbation and recovery tests, lifetime distributions, energy/material accounting, and comparison with simpler physical models.

## H2 — Some dissipative structures can become self-maintaining

**Claim:** A subset of non-equilibrium structures can use environmental flows to maintain the processes and constraints required for their continued existence.

**Predictions:** Removing access to the driving resource eventually causes loss of maintained organization; the structure channels flows in ways that sustain its persistence; measurable maintenance costs and failure modes exist.

**Nulls and alternatives:** The system is merely a passive pattern or transient event; persistence comes from a large initial stock of stored energy/material; or an external controller maintains the structure.

**Tests:** Resource interruption, maintenance-budget accounting, controlled perturbations, and comparison to passive pattern formation.

## H3 — Replication can arise from self-maintaining chemistry under some conditions

**Claim:** Under some physically plausible chemical and environmental conditions, self-maintaining structures can enter regimes of replication with heritable variation.

**Predictions:** Replication has identifiable chemical/material mechanisms and resource constraints; descendant states exhibit measurable continuity with parent states; variation is transmitted with sufficient fidelity for cumulative selection while allowing novelty.

**Nulls and alternatives:** Replication requires an externally specified template or machinery that already embodies the crucial organization; apparent reproduction is copying imposed by the algorithm; or replication occurs without heredity or cumulative evolution.

**Tests:** Mechanistically specified reaction networks, inheritance measures, mutation/variation experiments, lineage tracking and abiotic controls.

**Boundary:** A model that starts with replicators can investigate evolution, but cannot establish the origin of replication.

## H4 — Information processing can become selectively advantageous

**Claim:** In some environments, heritable mechanisms that improve sensing, memory, prediction or decision-making can increase long-term persistence or reproductive success enough to offset their costs.

**Predictions:** Such traits evolve only where information has predictive or decision value; their advantage decreases or reverses when signals are unreliable, action is costly, or environmental variability is too high or low; ablating the relevant information channel removes the advantage.

**Nulls and alternatives:** The apparent advantage is due to correlated traits or model artefacts; information costs exceed benefits; or simple non-informational strategies perform as well or better.

**Tests:** Matched information/no-information arms, signal shuffling, prediction-horizon ablations, cost sweeps and out-of-sample environmental changes.

## H5 — Agency is a selectable form of control under some conditions

**Claim:** Structures able to use information to select actions that change their future resource access or survival conditions can gain an advantage in some environments.

**Predictions:** Action benefits depend on controllable environmental consequences, not merely on a score assigned to action; agency-related traits evolve only when gains exceed sensing, computation, movement and actuation costs; benefits depend on environmental predictability, response time and the timescale of change.

**Nulls and alternatives:** Passive or fixed strategies perform equally well; action changes exposure but not net persistence; or apparent agency is generated by a pre-programmed policy that already contains the desired solution.

**Tests:** Matched passive controls, action ablations, cost sweeps, changed environments and explicit policy comparisons.

## H6 — Evolved organization changes planetary dissipation

**Claim:** Self-maintaining, replicating or agentic structures can alter planetary energy transformation and entropy production relative to matched abiotic conditions.

**Predictions:** Differences depend on physical properties such as reflectance, absorption, chemical storage, transport and radiative properties; sign and magnitude can vary by environment and organization; more biological throughput need not imply greater planetary entropy production.

**Nulls and alternatives:** Differences disappear when albedo, atmospheric and other physical effects are controlled; apparent change is due to boundary definition or incomplete energy accounting; or biotic systems alter where and when dissipation occurs without increasing the total under the chosen system boundary.

**Tests:** Matched external forcing, closed energy budgets, entropy-flow accounting, abiotic controls, sensitivity to optical/chemical properties and explicit system boundaries.

## Cross-cutting null — no general thermodynamic direction toward agency

Under a specified class of boundary conditions, increasingly sophisticated information processing and agency do not reliably emerge; simpler dissipative structures, passive strategies or non-agentic replicators may persist or perform as well or better.

A credible research programme must allow this outcome.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. **Conceptual consistency:** The claim does not obviously conflict with established physical principles.
2. **Mechanistic plausibility:** A physically specified mechanism exists.
3. **Model demonstration:** A transparent model exhibits the mechanism under stated assumptions.
4. **Robustness:** The outcome survives parameter sweeps, perturbations and alternative formulations.
5. **Empirical support:** Relevant observations or experiments support the mechanism.
6. **Generalization:** The result holds across a defensible range of systems and boundary conditions.

Evidence at one level must not be reported as evidence at a higher level.
