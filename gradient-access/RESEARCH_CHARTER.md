# Research Charter

## Governing question

Under what environmental and energetic conditions does heritable variation plus selection sustain access to kinetically difficult dissipative pathways better than passive or non-evolving alternatives?

## Governing hypothesis

In sufficiently constrained environments containing kinetically inaccessible free-energy gradients, systems with heritable variation and selection can sustain access to dissipative pathways that passive and non-evolving dissipative structures cannot access at comparable energetic and material costs.

## Scope

This project studies evolutionary mechanisms, not a unique thermodynamic property of life. It does not test whether abiogenesis is inevitable or whether entropy maximisation is an evolutionary objective.

## Operational comparisons

- N: uncatalysed passive baseline.
- M: fixed random catalyst library.
- D: self-maintaining population with faithful inheritance and no new mutation; selection can sort standing variation.
- L: heritable variation with ongoing mutation and selection.
- A: oracle-assisted non-replicating adaptive search, with explicit candidate-evaluation costs.
- Mmatched, Amatched and Umatched: fixed controls matched to L's paired late-window catalyst abundance.
- U: idealized target-matched designer benchmark.

## Independent quantities

Track reaction throughput, free-energy release, reaction entropy production, fuel-reservoir exchange entropy, catalyst abundance, construction/reproduction/maintenance/mutation/search costs, persistence and extinction separately.

## Falsification

The hypothesis is weakened if L does not outperform Mmatched and D in cold locked conditions; if its advantage disappears under equal energetic/material investment; if it depends on one narrow key geometry; or if Amatched reproduces the advantage at comparable costs.

## Evidence boundary

A simulation demonstrates only model behaviour under its declared assumptions. Empirical claims require chemically specified systems and independent experiments.
