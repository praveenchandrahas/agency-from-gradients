Preregistered in [`PREREGISTRATION_S14.md`](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/PREREGISTRATION_S14.md), frozen on `main` at commit `fe50486` before the run. 1,820 runs plus twins. [Actions run 43258823346](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/actions/runs/43258823346). Raw runs and the generated report are on the [`ci-results` branch](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/tree/ci-results/s14).

!!! abstract "Verdict"
    **H14a: not supported.** Copying appeared in 0 of 200 random chemistries. **H14b: not supported.** **H14c and H14d: not run**, because nothing emerged to test them on. All five gates passed.

## Read this before the numbers

This null is narrower than it looks.

I asked whether a driven soup starts to copy itself. The answer I got is to a smaller question.

**In none of the 200 chemistries was a closed set even possible.**

Not "possible and never lit". Not "lit and washed out". The catalysis tables did not contain one. Anywhere.

So the dynamics were never put to the test. The soup was not asked the question. That is a fault in the design, and it is mine.

## What was being tested

Everything before S14 starts with agents, genomes, birth and death. I put those in by hand.

S14 starts earlier. A flow reactor. Binary polymers up to six letters long. A fuel. A heat bath. Every reaction runs both ways and obeys detailed balance.

No agents. No genomes. No fitness. And no entropy rule anywhere in the dynamics.

One question: does anything start to copy itself? The reasoning is on [its own page](../before-life.md).

## Why zero?

I fixed one descriptive number in advance, for exactly this case. For each chemistry: does its catalysis table contain *any* closed set, ignoring counts and dynamics?

The answer: **0 of 200.**

So here is what happened. Catalysts were there. In 118 of the 200 chemistries, some polymer catalyses the very first fuel-coupled step, monomer plus monomer. But a closed set needs more. Every step back up to the catalyst must itself be catalysed by a member of the set. With one catalysed reaction per polymer, the loop never closes.

One chemistry got as far as spending 1% of its fuel in one run. That was the high-water mark.

(The 118 and the 1% are counts I made after the run. The 0 of 200 was fixed in advance.)

## How far off was the design?

!!! warning "Post hoc. Not preregistered."
    I ran this after seeing the result. It is an explanation, not a test.

I took 200 fresh chemistries (ids 2000 to 2199) and asked the same structural question at higher catalysis densities. No dynamics. Just: is a closed set possible?

| Catalysed joins per polymer, per kind | Closed set possible |
|---|---|
| **1** (the frozen value) | 0 of 200 |
| 2 | 60 of 200 |
| 3 | 165 of 200 |
| 5 | 198 of 200 |

It is a sharp threshold, and the frozen value sat just under it.

Where did "1" come from? From me. The classic work on autocatalytic sets finds them at about one to two catalysed reactions per molecule. But that work, as I read it, lets every molecule catalyse and treats dimers as food. Mine is stricter. Only polymers of three or more can catalyse, and food is monomers only. A stricter model has a higher threshold. I did not work that out before freezing.

Could I have? The structural scan needs no simulation. But it is the detector's first half, run on arm R's kind of chemistry. My own tuning rule forbade that before the run. I kept the rule. The price was a run that could not succeed.

And one caution. This table says where closed sets *can exist*. It says nothing about whether they would light, or survive washout, or pass the history test. That question is still untouched.

## What the controls showed

These are designed chemistries, ten runs each. They are not evidence that copying arises. They are worth reading anyway.

| | Copier (P) | Mineral (M) |
|---|---|---|
| Capture | 0.611 | 0.586 |
| Entropy rate | 8,070 | 8,077 |
| Polymer fraction | 0.685 | 0.651 |
| After sterilising | Dead for the full 100 residence times, in all 10 runs | Back within one residence time, in all 10 runs |
| Tuned to the drive? (shuffle percentile) | 0.964 | 0.494 |

Three things stand out.

**The detector can tell them apart.** Both are driven. Both build structure. Both dissipate. Only one is closed, and only one stays dead when wiped.

**The hurricane dissipates as much as the copier.** 8,077 against 8,070. The intervals overlap. Copying was not needed to burn the fuel. A fixed outside catalyst did it just as well.

I have to be careful here. The mineral's level is a number I chose. Set it higher and it would dissipate more. So this does not say which is the better entropy engine. It says only that, in this soup, copying is not what dissipation requires.

**Copying buys history at the price of mortality.** I predicted this one. Wipe the mineral soup and it is back at once, because its cause is outside. Wipe the copier and it is gone. The thing that can be inherited is the thing that can be lost.

## Cycling

Exploratory. A day-and-night feed with the same mean: 0 of 200, the same as the steady feed. With no closed set possible, nothing else could have happened.

## What this does and does not say

- It **does** show the engine is thermodynamically sound. The second law is exhibited, not imposed.
- It **does** show the detector separates a copier from a merely driven structure.
- It **does** show that at this catalysis density, in this family of chemistries, a closed set is not possible.
- It does **not** show that copying cannot arise in a driven soup. The run never reached that question.
- It does **not** say anything about early Earth.
- It does **not** bear on agency. S14 never measured it.

## What I expected, and what I got wrong

Before the run I wrote that H14a was "leaning not supported". The reason I gave was arithmetic: the coincidence a closed set needs is rare.

The verdict matched. The reasoning was too weak. I expected closed sets to be rare. They were absent. And they were absent for a structural reason I could have computed.

There was also a small leak in my blinding, recorded in the preregistration. Four pilot chemistries ran as fast as the no-catalysis arm, so I could tell nothing had taken off in them. It changed no parameter.

## What comes next

The preregistration allows one follow-up in this soup, as a new preregistration: a chemistry where a polymer helps assemble its own complement.

The post hoc table suggests a different one: a sweep of catalysis density, reported as a whole curve. That would be more informative. It is also exactly what my preregistration said I would not do, "tune until it appears", unless it is filed in advance as a sweep and not as a rescue.

I have not decided. H13, in the pond world, is next in line either way.
