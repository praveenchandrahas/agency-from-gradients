# S8: what if entropy IS the objective?

Preregistered in [`PREREGISTRATION_S8.md`](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/PREREGISTRATION_S8.md). 5 optimisation runs per kappa, 8 held-out worlds each. Raw runs are on the [`ci-results` branch](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/tree/ci-results).

!!! abstract "Verdict"
    H8a and H8c **supported**. H8b **not supported** at this power. An explicit entropy goal selects for burning energy. Not for anything I would call agency.

## The reverse of everything else

Every other experiment *refuses* to reward entropy. S8 does the opposite, on purpose.

One shared controller. All 60 life forms run the same genome. It is optimised, with evolution strategies, directly for the world's total entropy production. Life forms eat and scavenge as before. But nothing rewards survival or reproduction.

Then I ask: what behaviour does the objective produce?

!!! note "These are not blind predictions"
    S8 was preregistered *after* a two-run-per-kappa pilot. The pilot informed the hypotheses and the fixed parameters. Weigh them accordingly.

## Results at kappa = 3

| Controller | Entropy rate | Capture fraction | Big-deposit share | Actions spent investing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Optimised for entropy** (mean of 5 runs) | 35.8 [29.5, 40.1] | 0.88 | 0.33 | 0.54 |
| Hand-coded forager | 29.0 | 0.49 | 0.63 | 0.27 |
| Random controller | 19.0 | 0.42 | 0.06 | 0.13 |
| Always move | 20.3 | 0.55 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| No life forms | 11.8 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

| Hypothesis | Claim | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H8a** | Optimised beats the hand-coded forager on entropy rate | +6.85 [0.58, 11.11] | **Supported** |
| **H8b** | Optimised spends more of its actions investing (a "furnace") | +0.27 [-0.01, 0.44] | **Not supported**. The interval just reaches zero |
| **H8c** | The gain is larger at kappa = 3 than at kappa = 1 | +12.96 [6.58, 17.54] | **Supported** |

## What the optimiser actually did

**Four of five runs found the same solution. One did not.**

In the four, the controller captured about 90% of the energy. It held the big deposits open. It spent 59 to 73% of its actions investing. Fitness 36 to 41.

The fifth run never found the deposits. Fitness 23.6. No investing.

With only five runs, the spread matters more than the mean. And that failed run is exactly what pulls H8b's interval to zero.

## It beats my best hand-written forager

On the objective itself, yes.

Higher capture (0.88 against 0.49). A larger surviving population (57 against 42). But a *smaller* share of energy from big deposits (0.33 against 0.63).

Why? The objective rewards burning energy. Much of its edge is the heat from constant investing. So it is closer to a **furnace** than a forager.

## At kappa = 1, there is little to optimise

As predicted. The gain over random is about 3.9 (0.8 to 5.6). Two of five runs ended with the population nearly gone.

H8c is the preregistered way of saying this. **The objective's power comes from the kappa assumption.**

Recall how entropy is counted: [leak plus kappa times heat](../world.md#the-accounting-assumption). Raise kappa, and burning energy pays more by definition. So S8 reveals which behaviour maximises an *assumed* quantity. It does not show the assumption holds.

## Why this matters for the thesis

The objective can be gamed. That is the point of testing it.

An explicit entropy goal selects for burning energy. It does not select for anything we would call agency.

So there is a good reason to keep entropy as something measured, never rewarded. The design rule is not a stylistic choice. This result is the evidence for it.

!!! warning "A caveat on the caveat"
    A shared controller sidesteps the free-rider problem that individual selection faces. It removes cooperation costs by construction. So the comparison with S2 and S6 is not like for like.
