# S6: the free-rider test

Preregistered in [`PREREGISTRATION_S6.md`](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/PREREGISTRATION_S6.md). 30 seeds per cell.

!!! abstract "Verdict"
    **Not supported at this power.** The simple free-rider story is not confirmed.

## The question

H2 failed. My explanation, formed *after* seeing the data, was a public-goods problem.

Opening a deposit costs the investor. The benefit is split evenly with everyone on the square. So selection, under fatal starvation and inheritance, punishes the investor.

If that is right, there is an obvious fix. Make the benefit **private**.

## The manipulation

A new sharing rule, `contributor`. The harvest on a deposit is split by each agent's recent investment, not evenly. Total harvest is unchanged.

- Each agent keeps an investment trace. It decays at 0.98 per step and gains 1 for each invest action.
- Non-investors get a small floor (0.02), so nobody is shut out entirely.

Everything else is the full world. Heredity is crossed with the sharing rule: four conditions. The default rule, `equal`, reproduces S1 to S5 bit for bit. I checked that on seed 0.

!!! note "A change of metric"
    The main run showed that `t2_reached` is coarse. So the primary metric here is `share_tier2`: the steady-state share of energy from big deposits. `t2_reached` is reported alongside.

## The hypotheses

**H6a.** Private benefit increases steady use of big deposits under heredity.

**H6b.** Heredity becomes load-bearing once investing pays privately.

## Results

| | Contrast | `share_tier2` | `t2_reached` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H6a** | Contributor minus equal sharing, heredity on | +0.024 [-0.023, 0.075] | +0.13 [-0.10, 0.37] |
| **H6b** | Heredity minus no heredity, contributor rule | -0.017 [-0.059, 0.032] | -0.60 [-0.77, -0.43] |

Both hypotheses **not supported**.

## How to read this

**H6a** points the right way. The interval includes zero. So: not shown at this power. It is not a refutation.

**H6b** is the harder one. Heredity *still* does not help. Random fresh genomes still reach big deposits in 100% of runs. Inherited ones lag far behind (-0.60).

So something else is going on. Whatever makes inherited policies avoid, or fail to sustain, the big deposits, a private payoff did not fix it.

## What it might be

Three candidates. All untested.

1. **The policy class is too constrained.** A 27-gene policy may be unable to learn a conditional, cooperative rule: "invest only when others are nearby".
2. **The cost and the payoff arrive too far apart** for a policy this small to link them.
3. **The extrinsic hazard dominates.** Agents die for reasons that have nothing to do with their choices.

This is the reason the next step was a richer world and a bigger genome. That became [S7](s7.md).

!!! info "What a positive result would have meant"
    Even a clean H6a would have said only this: making the benefit private lets selection find big deposits *in this world*. It would not have said Earth's agency arose that way.
