# S9: a learned private reward

Preregistered in [`PREREGISTRATION_S9.md`](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/PREREGISTRATION_S9.md). Plain world v2. 100,000 steps. 20 seeds per arm. No extinctions. Raw runs are on the [`ci-results` branch](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/tree/ci-results).

!!! abstract "Verdict"
    **H9a, H9b, H9c, H9d: not supported.** The reversed signs on two of them are a **probe confound**, not better agents.

## The reasoning

S8 showed that an explicit entropy objective gives a furnace.

But life does not reward entropy as such. It gives individuals *learned internal signals* tied to energy and homeostasis. Hunger. Satiety. That sort of thing.

So S9 asks the engineering question. If every agent learns a private reward during its lifetime, does anything we would call agency appear? Selection stays persistence-only in every arm.

## The three arms

| Arm | Reward signal |
|---|---|
| **Off** | No learning |
| **Energy** | The agent's own energy change: intake minus cost. A life-like homeostatic signal |
| **Heat** | The heat the agent dissipates. An explicit private entropy reward |

Each agent adds a small learned correction to its output layer, trained online by a reward-modulated policy gradient. The correction is **reset at birth**. Nothing learned is inherited.

That last point matters. It means S9 does not test a Baldwin effect.

## Two agency criteria, fixed in advance

1. **A predictive probe.** How much does adding the hidden state improve prediction of the agent's own energy gain over the next 20 steps, compared with random genomes?
2. **A viability probe.** Recovery after displacement and depletion, trained minus random. The same idea as S4.

## Results

| Arm | Entropy rate | Tier-2 share | Predictive probe (trained minus random) | Viability recovery (trained minus random) | Evolved recovery | Random recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No learning | 100.8 | 0.026 | 0.024 | 0.134 | 0.480 | 0.346 |
| Learn from energy | 115.5 | 0.117 | 0.027 | 0.058 | 0.457 | 0.399 |
| Learn from heat | 109.6 | 0.053 | 0.027 | 0.238 | 0.458 | 0.220 |

| Hypothesis | Claim | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **H9a** | Energy learning raises the predictive probe | +0.003 [-0.025, 0.032]. Not supported |
| **H9b** | Heat learning raises the predictive probe | +0.002 [-0.022, 0.029]. Not supported |
| **H9c** | Energy learning raises recovery | -0.077 [-0.144, -0.012]. Not supported, **sign reversed** |
| **H9d** | Heat is worse than energy for recovery | +0.180 [0.114, 0.245]. Not supported, **sign reversed** |

## The reversed signs are not what they look like

Look at the last two columns of the results table.

Evolved agents recover about equally well in *every* arm: 0.46 to 0.48. They do not change.

What moves is the **random baseline**. Learning from energy helps naive random agents (0.35 up to 0.40). Learning from heat hurts them (0.35 down to 0.22).

So the "delta" measures how much the learning rule helps or hurts a *random* agent. It does not measure how agent-like the evolved population is.

I should have seen this before preregistering. I did not. The preregistered contrast is reported as registered, and I am recording the flaw here instead of quietly choosing a better metric.

## The predictive probe is too weak to settle anything

It sits near zero in every arm. And it has **no positive control** in this study.

So a null here cannot separate two readings:

1. There is no predictive information in the hidden state.
2. The probe is too weak to see it.

The probe is linear. It targets only the agent's own energy gain. I cannot tell which reading is right.

## Secondary findings

Descriptive. Not preregistered as tests.

- **Both learning arms raised the entropy rate.** Energy: +14.7 [9.3, 19.8]. Heat: +8.8 [3.4, 13.9].
- **Energy learning raised the tier-2 share:** +0.091 [0.031, 0.149]. Heat learning did not: +0.027 [-0.025, 0.078].
- **Heat did not beat energy at producing entropy:** -5.9 [-11.2, -0.7].

That last one is telling. The explicit entropy reward was no better at producing entropy than the life-like signal.

It repeats the S8 lesson, this time at the level of the individual.
