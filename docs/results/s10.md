# S10: dissipative adaptation with no selection

Preregistered in [`PREREGISTRATION_S10.md`](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/PREREGISTRATION_S10.md). No births. No deaths. 60 agents. 100,000 steps. 20 seeds per arm. Raw runs are on the [`ci-results` branch](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/tree/ci-results).

!!! abstract "Verdict"
    **H10a to c supported, but by construction.** **H10d and H10e not supported.** With the bias imposed, the system absorbs more energy. It does not climb the ladder.

## What is being tested

England's argument, in my words: in a driven system sitting in a heat bath, rearrangements are biased by the work absorbed and dissipated along the way. So structures that absorb and dissipate drive become more likely. No selection for reproduction needed.

Is that enough to climb the energy ladder here?

## The simulation

Take selection away entirely. No births. No deaths. No fitness.

The only thing that changes a policy is a **rearrangement rule**. Each agent alternates 100-step windows. It scores its current genome, then a proposed random rearrangement. It keeps the new one with probability

\[
p = \min\!\left(1,\; e^{\beta r}\right), \qquad \beta = 10
\]

where \(r\) is the relative change in the score.

What counts as the score depends on the arm.

| Arm | Score |
|---|---|
| **Absorbed** | Energy taken in during the window |
| **Dissipated** | Heat dissipated, capped by what was absorbed |
| **Neutral** | Keep with probability 1/2, regardless. The drift control |
| **Anti** | The opposite bias: \(e^{-\beta r}\) |
| **Forager** | The hand-coded forager. Headroom only |

The cap on the dissipated arm has a reason. Bailed-out agents get free energy. Without a cap, that injected energy would count as "dissipating the drive".

## The honest framing

Read this before the numbers.

The rule is a local-detailed-balance idea written as an acceptance rule. It **assumes** rearrangements are biased by absorbed or dissipated work.

Because I impose the bias, an increase in the biased quantity is expected *by construction*. It is stochastic hill-climbing without selection.

So S10 cannot show that matter obeys the rule. Its informative question is narrower: does the biased rule *climb the ladder*? That needs coordinated, delayed payoffs, which hill-climbing on intake does not obviously supply.

## Results

| Arm | Intake per agent-step (late) | Tier-2 share of intake |
|---|---|---|
| Bias toward absorbed work | 0.200 | 0.476 |
| Bias toward dissipated work (capped) | 0.205 | 0.497 |
| Neutral | 0.163 | 0.510 |
| Opposite bias | 0.110 | 0.618 |
| Hand-coded forager | 0.033 | 0.701 |

| Hypothesis | Contrast | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H10a** | Absorbed minus neutral, intake | +0.036 [0.023, 0.049] | Supported (by construction) |
| **H10b** | Dissipated minus neutral, intake | +0.041 [0.031, 0.051] | Supported (by construction) |
| **H10c** | Absorbed minus opposite, intake | +0.089 [0.074, 0.104] | Supported (by construction) |
| **H10d** | Absorbed minus neutral, tier-2 share | -0.033 [-0.070, 0.004] | **Not supported** |
| **H10e** | Dissipated minus neutral, tier-2 share | -0.013 [-0.050, 0.023] | **Not supported** |

H10a to c are manipulation checks. A dose-response is exactly what a hill-climbing rule gives. It is not evidence about physics.

H10d and H10e are the substantive ones. They failed.

## The odd result

Look at the *opposite* bias. It had the **highest** tier-2 share of any arm with a rearrangement rule: 0.62.

And the hand-coded forager scores worst on intake (0.033), while scoring best on tier-2 share (0.70).

Why?

Per-agent intake favours grazing on the commons. The forager invests heavily. So its per-agent intake is low, even though most of its energy comes from the deposits.

So per-agent intake **rewards the wrong behaviour for this world**. A rule that maximises it drifts *away* from the ladder.

I have not tested why the opposite bias ends up highest. The above explains why intake is a poor yardstick. It does not explain that specific number.

I report this as found. I did not explain it away.

## A deviation to note

H10e was preregistered. I never coded it into the analysis script. The number above comes from the script's descriptive line. I recorded the omission in the preregistration file.

## Bottom line

With the bias imposed, the system absorbs more energy than under drift. But it does not climb the ladder. And nothing agent-like follows from that alone.

!!! info "On England's own claim"
    This does not refute England. It tests a specific, imposed version of the rule in one toy world. The original result is about the statistical physics of driven systems. This is a different thing.
