# S15: Locks. Does life open the lock when nothing else does?

!!! abstract "The short version"
    **No. Not in this model.**

    Life beat the passive world, the mineral and the fire at every cold barrier. Its lead grew as the lock got harder. With no lock, it added nothing. All as predicted.

    But something else opened the lock too. A fire with a decent starting key held it open at 78% of the designer's level. A mineral bed a hundred times bigger matched life outright.

    What survives is narrower. **Life is the only structure here that makes its key better.** That takes it to the designer's level with about a hundred times less material than a random mineral bed. But it never opened a lock that something non-living could not at least hold open at the start.

## It went the way I wrote down

Before the run, from arithmetic alone, I expected:

- the fire to open the lock at key length 24, with about 560 catalysts (it had 553),
- life to reach about 1,185 (it did),
- the mineral to sit near 280 and 60 (it sat at 290 and 44),
- the fire to mostly stall at key length 32 (it spent 11% of the fuel).

That is a good sign for the engine. It is also a warning. Most of this outcome was visible in the arithmetic before anything ran. The model did what its numbers said it would.

## What I did not preregister

These came from looking at the runs afterwards. Treat them as leads, not findings.

### 1. The fire sorts. Only life invents.

At key length 24, the fire's best key at the end was exactly the best key in its starting library, in all 20 worlds. Selection sorted what was there. Nothing new appeared.

Life ended within 0.03 bits of the perfect key on average, in every surviving world. Its neutral marker bits drifted to about 12 bits from their target, which is what chance gives. So the keys moved because they were selected, not because they drifted.

### 2. Life climbs. It does not start.

At key length 32, look at each world's fate:

| | Worlds |
|---|---|
| Life and fire both survive | 13 |
| Only life survives | **0** |
| Only the fire survives | 2 |
| Neither survives | 5 |

Life never survived where the fire died.

Where life did survive, it always climbed all the way to the perfect key. But it needed a foothold first: a starting key just good enough to live on. Without one, it died with the fire. In two worlds it died *where the fire lived*. Its copying errors thinned out a key that was barely good enough. The fire, copying exactly, held on.

Variation is what lets life climb. At the edge, it is also what kills it.

### 3. Size can stand in for life

At the coldest barrier, I also ran minerals with 10 and 100 times life's number of catalysts.

| | Key length 24 | Key length 32 |
|---|---|---|
| Mineral, 1x | 290 | 44 |
| Mineral, 10x | 927 | 319 |
| Mineral, 100x | 1,174 | 952 |
| Life | 1,185 | 771 |

A big enough random rock surface does the job. At key length 32, the 100x mineral was even above life on average: +181 [−57, 420], an interval that includes zero. Life died in 7 worlds. Rocks don't die.

### 4. No lock, nothing to do

H2c passed, but trivially. At barriers 0 and 2, every fire and every life world went extinct. The passive route spent the fuel first. Nothing was left to live on. So "life adds nothing" held because there was no life.

## What this does to the claim

The claim was:

> In cold, locked gradients, where passive structures and non-living dissipative ones stall, life opens the lock. Nothing else does.

In this model, "nothing else does" is false. A fire with a decent key opens it. A large enough mineral bed opens it.

What survives is a different claim:

> **Among structures of the same size, only life improves its key. So it reaches the best possible dissipation with far less material. But it needs a foothold that non-living matter already provides.**

That is not "life is the only thing that drains cold gradients". It is "life is the thing that gets *better* at draining them".

That fits the thesis's succession idea better than the original claim did. The fire holds the lock slightly open. Life gets in through the gap and widens it.

## What this does not show

- **It is one toy chemistry.** How rare a working key is was set by the key length, and I chose it. A chemistry where no random structure fits at all would leave life with no foothold either.
- **Copying was given.** Where the first copier comes from is not asked.
- **The lock never moved.** A drifting lock is where a fixed key should fail and an evolving one should not. That is H3a. It is not tested yet.
- **Only one lock.** Opening a *sequence* of locks, each built on the last, is H3b. Also not tested.
- **Nothing here is about agency.**

## What comes next

Two things, in this order.

1. **The drifting lock (H3a).** If life's real edge is that it improves, a lock that changes is where that edge should show. A fire's fixed key goes stale. A mineral bed would need to be enormous to cover every lock the drift visits.
2. **Stacked locks (H3b).** Does life ratchet up through locks no fixed structure reaches?

Both need their own preregistration, with the same blind rule. The expectations should again be written down first, from arithmetic.
