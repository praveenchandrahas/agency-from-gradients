# S16, gradient access: evolution against matched controls

!!! warning "Status: exploratory, as of 2026-11-23"
    Nothing here is preregistered. The v0.1 run failed its own audit. The v0.2 runs are exploratory. The confirmatory test is v0.3, not yet built.

S16 began as a separate repository, `evolutionary-gradient-access`. It was folded in here on 2026-11-23, with its history, as [`gradient-access/`](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/tree/main/gradient-access).

## Why it exists

[S15](s15.md) matched its mineral to life's catalyst *count*. That's fairer than a number I picked. But it isn't equal *investment*.

Life builds its catalysts by spending free energy. It pays to copy, to mutate, to maintain. A fixed mineral pays none of that. So "life beats the mineral" or "the mineral matches life" are both comparisons at unequal cost.

S16 asks the fairer question: at equal energy and material investment, does evolving beat not evolving?

## The arms

The same letters as S15, plus a search control.

| Arm | What it is |
|---|---|
| N | No catalyst |
| M, Mmatched | Fixed random library; Mmatched sized to the evolving arm's late population |
| D, Dmatched | Copies faithfully, no new mutation. Selection can sort what it starts with |
| L | Copies with mutation. The evolving arm |
| U, Umatched | A designer's perfect keys |
| A, Amatched | Oracle-assisted search that does not replicate |

## v0.1: audited and withdrawn

2,000 runs: 10 arms, 5 environments, 4 barriers, 16-bit keys, 10 paired seeds.

Its headline was that L beat matched fixed libraries at non-zero barriers. Its own audit then found four blocking problems:

1. The energy spent on reproduction was never added up, so every "total investment" figure is wrong.
2. Energy for reproduction used a constant, not the actual reaction affinity.
3. The exchange entropy left out the waste species. That's the same class of mistake as S15's mixing lesson.
4. Populations hit the 500-catalyst cap within one or two steps. So v0.1 never tested persistence under a tight energy budget at all.

The raw throughput outputs stand. The cost comparisons do not. [Full audit](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/gradient-access/VALIDATION_AUDIT.md).

## v0.2: the stronger controls

A rebuilt model with coupled chemistry. Its free-energy identity closes to numerical precision. 160 runs, 32 paired conditions, static and drifting targets.

Mean ratio of the evolving arm L to each control:

| | Static: Mmatched | Drift: Mmatched | Static: Dmatched | Drift: Dmatched |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cumulative fuel converted | 1.041 | 0.934 | 0.819 | 0.758 |
| Work invested | 1.212 | 1.200 | 0.733 | 0.725 |
| Fuel converted per unit of work | 0.859 | 0.780 | 1.117 | 1.046 |

Read it this way:

- **Against a fixed library with the same catalyst material,** L converted less fuel under drift and spent about 20% more work doing it.
- **Against a large faithful-copying library,** L converted less in total. It was slightly more efficient per unit of work, but the large library also spent more.
- **Mutation helped** only against a *small* copying population that started from the same library.

The repository's own conclusion: v0.2 "does not establish that ongoing mutation and selection outperform strong fixed or standing-variation alternatives at comparable energetic and material costs." [Full report](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/gradient-access/results/V02_STANDING_CONTROLS.md).

This points the same way as S15's H2e: a big enough non-evolving library gets there too.

## One design flaw it found

In v0.2, a catalyst's key directly speeds its own copying. That builds part of the answer in.

The fix: a lineage's captured energy pays for that lineage's copying, through private pools. Keys affect capture, not copying. That work, `gradient-access/src/ega/model_v03.py` with its design and tests, is on the branch `s16/v03-lineage-energy`, under review. It has not run any experiment yet.

S15 should be checked for the same weakness. Its catalysts grow from their share of the reaction's free energy, which is closer to the fix. But I haven't checked it line by line.

## v0.3: the test that matters

The [equal-investment design](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/gradient-access/MODEL_V03_EQUAL_BUDGET_DESIGN.md) charges every arm for the work it starts with and the work it spends. It then compares arms on a shared budget frontier.

Two questions run there, once:

- **H16-2:** does evolving beat not evolving at equal cost?
- **H16-4, which is S15's H3a:** does an evolving key track a drifting lock better than fixed or standing libraries, at equal cost?

It gets preregistered before it runs, like S15.
