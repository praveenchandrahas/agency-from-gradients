# S11: the best way to raise the entropy of a lifeless universe

Preregistered in [`PREREGISTRATION_S11.md`](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/PREREGISTRATION_S11.md). 20 held-out parameter draws, three horizons, 64 jobs. Raw runs are on the [`ci-results` branch](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/tree/ci-results).

!!! abstract "Verdict"
    **H11a, c, e supported. H11b, d not. H11f, h mixed.** A consistency check on published formulas, not a discovery.

How the model works is on [its own page](../cosmos.md).

## The answer

Build starlight collectors for energy. Then feed gas into black holes and merge them.

That strategy gains about \(10^{110}\,k_B\) over doing nothing, at 100 Gyr, \(10^{12}\) yr and \(10^{14}\) yr. More than 99.99% of it is black-hole entropy, from the area law \(S \propto M^2\).

Does that surprise anyone? It should not. The literature already says black holes dominate cosmic entropy. Matching it is a consistency check on the formulas.

## Hypotheses

| | Claim | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **H11a** | Best strategy has positive gain at every horizon, dominated by black holes | **Supported** at all horizons |
| **H11b** | More time means more gain: 1e14 yr beats 100 Gyr | **Not supported**: -0.21 [-0.48, 0.06] |
| **H11c** | Removing the reach limit raises the best gain | **Supported**: +2.2 to +2.4 decades |
| **H11d** | An open-ended neural search beats the best hand-designed strategy | **Not supported**: 0.5 to 1.4 decades *worse* |
| **H11e** | Disabling the black hole levers costs more than 5 decades | **Supported**, expected by construction |
| **H11f** | Without the radiator-area limit, collectors rival the black hole route | **Mixed**: only at 1e14 yr, and flagged unphysical |
| **H11g** | Scaling the black hole coefficient by 0.1 or 10 shifts gain by about 1 decade | Within tolerance |
| **H11h** | Halving or doubling costs changes gain by less than 0.5 decades | Supported at 1e11 and 1e12, not at 1e14 |

The priors for H11d and H11e were written down before the pilots. H11d's "not supported" matches what I expected.

## What the search found, and did not

**The open-ended search found nothing the levers missed.** The neural policy came out 0.5 to 1.4 decades *worse*. Whatever is in the lever set is what matters.

**No time gain after 100 Gyr** in this model. H11b is not supported. What sets the magnitude is how much matter you can reach, and how much sits in each bound domain. That is H11c.

**Costs matter at 10 times and above.** At the default calibration they are cheap against stellar energy. The 1000 times arm shows where they bind.

**Removing the radiator-area limit opens a photon route.** It reaches \(10^{117.7}\) at \(10^{14}\) yr. That is unphysical. The model flags it. I do not read it as a result.

## The exploit audit

Optimising against a model is a good way to find the model's bugs. So I planned an audit.

1. Ledger tests inside every rollout. Reject and penalise any violation.
2. Inspect the best policies' action shares over time.
3. Re-evaluate each best policy with costs at 10 and 100 times, with accretion energy not capturable, with no radiator relaxation, and with the domain floor on and off.
4. Any exploit found gets fixed in the model, re-run, and logged.

What it found:

- **No ledger violation.** Maximum relative error, \(2.5\times10^{-15}\).
- **One exploit, in the pilot.** Hole mass was unbounded by domain size. Fixed before the confirmatory run.

## What it means for the thesis

Here is the point of the whole experiment.

**The entropy-maximal structure is a black hole. The optimal controller is an engineer.**

An entropy objective alone does not favour life or intelligence. By the model's own accounting, the best way to raise entropy has nothing to do with agency.

But hold the caveat tightly. The model excludes life *by assumption*. So this cannot say whether life raises entropy. It says only that a pure entropy-maximiser, given the options in this ledger, would not build it.

That fits what [S8](s8.md) found in the pond. An entropy goal gives a furnace. Here it gives a black hole.

## The deviations, logged

Six, listed in the preregistration. Two to know:

- The seed work reservoir had to be raised to \(10^{-3}\,M_\odot c^2\). At \(10^{-15}\) the collectors could not bootstrap.
- Differences below about \(10^{89}\,k_B\) in the \(\sim 3\times10^{104}\) total are floating-point noise. Gains under \(10^{90}\) are set to zero in the objective and the analysis.
