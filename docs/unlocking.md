# Civilizations and locks: catching against unlocking

!!! warning "Status: a proposal, as of 2026-11-23"
    Nothing on this page is preregistered. It records where the Kardashev link stands after one day's discussion, a two-line calculation, and a plan for tests. Numbers marked *to check* are from memory.

## Where this comes from

My original chain ended with civilizations. Capable structures use more of a star's power, and the Kardashev scale measures that. I wanted to know if that link survives the [revised hypothesis](standing.md#the-revised-hypothesis).

Before that, one broader claim needs fixing.

"Everything in the Universe follows from one principle: everything wants to increase entropy." I have said this. It is not right as stated.

- **Energy is conserved.** Nothing lowers the Universe's energy. A system lowers its own energy only by moving energy out, where it raises entropy elsewhere. Hot hydrogen stays ionized because, at high temperature, entropy beats the binding energy.
- **The second law constrains. It does not generate.** It says what cannot happen, not what does, or how fast. A heat-dead Universe obeys it. So does one full of stars.
- **It needs two things it cannot supply.** A low-entropy start. And the delays: hydrogen the early Universe expanded too fast to fuse, stars that burn slowly, fuel that sits next to oxygen. All the interesting structure lives in the delays. That is what [Locks](locks.md) is about.
- **It cannot be wrong.** Every observation fits it. So it is a frame, not a hypothesis.

The version I keep:

> Every macroscopic process is the Universe running down from a low-entropy start. The second law sets the direction. Locks set the pace. Stars, storms and living things are ways the locks get opened. Life is the kind that gets better at opening them.

And one word to drop: "wants". Entropy follows. It is not the goal.

## Not new

Dyson described the locks in 1971. He called them "hang-ups": a balance of gravity, nuclear reactions and radiation that keeps energy from flowing too fast ([Energy in the Universe](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/energy-in-the-universe/), *Scientific American*). In 1960 he proposed looking for civilizations by their infrared waste heat.

Kleidon argues that technology could raise a planet's total free-energy generation, not just consume it ([arXiv:1103.2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.2014)). Frank, Grinspoon and Walker treat technological intelligence as a planetary-scale transition, with immature and mature technospheres ([Int. J. Astrobiology 2022](https://doi.org/10.1017/S147355042100029X)). Wright's group and Project Hephaistos search for Dyson spheres by their waste heat ([MNRAS 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae1186)).

## Two claims, not one

"Civilizations raise entropy" hides two different claims.

| | Claim | Needs life? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Catching** | A civilization absorbs starlight and re-radiates it colder, so entropy production rises | No. Any cold absorber does it | Settled thermodynamics. Whether civilizations do it is a question for astronomy |
| **Unlocking** | A civilization releases locked free energy faster than nature would | Possibly. That is the claim | Speculative. Tests below |

The Kardashev scale measures power used. That is catching. A Type II civilization captures its star's output, but the star already did the unlocking: gravity squeezed the hydrogen until it fused. Under a fixed flux like starlight, no catcher can release more than the star sends.

## Catching: the Dyson sphere calculation

Starlight streaming into empty space keeps its entropy. Nothing new is made after it leaves the star.

An absorber that re-radiates the same power as a black body at temperature $T_d$ emits entropy at

$$\dot S_{\text{out}} = \frac{4}{3}\,\frac{L}{T_d}.$$

The starlight carried $\frac{4}{3} L / T_\odot$. So the absorber raises the entropy flow by roughly $T_\odot / T_d$.

For the Sun ($L = 3.83 \times 10^{26}$ W, $T_\odot = 5772$ K):

| Absorber T (K) | Entropy out (W/K) | Ratio to starlight | Shell radius (AU) | Max work fraction (Landsberg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 1.70 × 10²⁴ | 19 | 1.7 | 0.931 |
| 100 | 5.10 × 10²⁴ | 58 | 15.5 | 0.977 |
| 50 | 1.02 × 10²⁵ | 115 | 62 | 0.988 |
| 2.725 | 1.87 × 10²⁶ | 2,118 | 20,865 | 0.999 |

Generated by [`analysis/dyson_entropy.py`](https://github.com/praveenchandrahas/agency-from-gradients/blob/main/analysis/dyson_entropy.py). Entropy *produced* is the ratio minus one, in units of the starlight's entropy.

So yes: a Dyson sphere raises entropy production. About 19 times the starlight's own entropy at 300 K.

**But a dust shell does the same.** The books see only two temperatures: the one energy arrives at and the one it leaves at. A civilization may run computers on the energy in between. That work still ends as heat. A dust shell at 300 K with the same covering fraction gives exactly the same entropy. That is why Dyson-sphere searches struggle: dust mimics the signal.

**And nature already does some of it.** In real galaxies a large share of starlight is absorbed by dust and re-radiated in the infrared. The share is *to check*. The gain over what nature already does is smaller than the table.

**What a civilization could change:** the absorber's temperature, and how much of the sky it covers. A civilization that wants work, not entropy, has a reason to build cold. The work fraction rises as the absorber gets colder. Entropy production rises with it, as a side effect. That fits the revised hypothesis: entropy follows. The limit is cost, because a colder shell must be larger.

## Unlocking: how to test it

The full claim, that civilizations elsewhere unlock cosmic gradients, cannot be tested here. We have one civilization and no observations of any other. But it splits into parts, and each part can fail.

A measure first. For each lock:

> **Unlock factor** = release rate with a civilization ÷ release rate without one.

### Test 1. A lock inventory

Desk research, days.

List every lock: hydrogen in gas that never forms stars, deuterium, fissile uranium, fossil carbon, black-hole spin. For each: the stock, the natural process that opens it if any, and its rate.

**Fails if** every lock has a natural opener at a comparable rate. Then "only civilizations" becomes "faster than nature".

One lock already fails. Black-hole spin has a natural opener: the [Blandford–Znajek process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blandford%E2%80%93Znajek_process), the leading explanation for astrophysical jets. Whether jets really tap the hole's spin is still debated. Either way, "only civilizations open black-hole spin" is unsafe.

Hydrogen in gas that never forms stars may be the real candidate. As far as I know, nothing natural fuses it.

### Test 2. Earth's lock ledger

Real data, days. The one place we have a civilization to measure.

| Lock | Natural release | Human release | Unlock factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fossil (rock) carbon | 68 MtC per year by weathering ([Zondervan et al., *Nature* 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06581-9)) | About 10 GtC per year (*to check*, Global Carbon Project) | About 150 |
| Uranium-235 | Decay, half-life about 700 million years | A reactor burns its fuel in a few years | Roughly 10⁸ per atom (*to check*) |

These are measurements, not speculation. For one planet, "civilization opens locks far faster than nature" already holds.

A twist: life also *made* one of these locks. Fossil carbon exists because living things buried it.

### Test 3. The lock ladder

A preregistered simulation in the [S15 engine](locks.md). Weeks.

S15 left a lead: life needed a foothold. Mutation and selection climb only where partial keys pay a little. Some locks give no partial payoff. Fusion is the clearest: nothing comes back until ignition.

**Hypothesis.** What civilizations add is opening locks *without a foothold*. They spend stored free energy on search that pays nothing until it succeeds.

**Design.**

- A ladder of locks, rising in barrier and in stock. The deepest gives zero return until the key is nearly exact.
- The S15 arms: passive, mineral (including a large library), fire, life.
- A new **investor** arm. It can bank free energy and spend it on search with no immediate return.

**Predictions.**

1. Only the investor opens the footholdless lock.
2. Only when the lock's stock exceeds what the search costs. That threshold is the prediction that can fail.
3. Persistence decides the end. A fast unlocker that drains the shallow locks before it reaches the deep one dies out.

**Fails if** life opens the footholdless lock too, given time; or the investor never earns back its search cost; or a large mineral library opens it, as one matched life in S15.

**By-construction warning.** An investor with unlimited random search finds the key eventually. The informative results are the cost, the threshold, and whether anything survives the climb. Those get written down before any run.

This is also where Kolchinsky's bound and the agency step meet. Agency, here, is the ability to pay for information before it pays back.

### Test 4. What unlocking would look like from outside

Desk research, then a question for Wright's group.

Unlocking leaves different traces from catching:

- a star emitting more than its fusion can supply;
- fusion products outside stars;
- black holes spinning down faster than their jets explain;
- infrared waste heat with no star to account for it.

We cannot observe these. We can list them and check which surveys already set limits.

## Order

1. Tests 1 and 2. Cheap, real data. Test 1 may narrow "only civilizations" to "faster than nature" before anything is built.
2. Test 3, preregistered like S15.
3. Test 4, as the thing to bring to Wright.

None of this shows that civilizations elsewhere unlock anything. That stays speculative, and only observation can bound it. The tests can show whether the claim is coherent, true for the one case we have, and distinctive in the way I say.
