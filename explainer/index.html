An explainer, with live simulations

Where does the leftover energy go?

Stars burn fast and waste most of what they make. Life is what grew to catch the scraps. This page asks a simple question: if we want an artificial mind, should we copy the conditions that produced life, instead of handing it a goal?

One honest note first: these are small toy worlds. They test an idea. They do not prove anything about Earth.

1 · The idea

Energy always spills.

Think of water falling down a staircase of pools. Each pool catches some and lets the rest spill over. What spills past the last pool is gone into the dark. Move the slider to add more mills that catch the spill.

2 · The live pond

Now give creatures the same conditions.

This pond runs the real model from our research code. Creatures eat the glowing grass. They also find small wells and big wells, which stay shut unless creatures spend energy to hold them open. Wasted energy rises away as embers. Nobody is told what to want: creatures that gather enough energy have children, those that starve disappear, and children inherit their parent's instincts with small random changes. Press Very fast and watch.

3 · What we found

Mostly not what we predicted.

We wrote down our predictions before running anything, then ran 30 ponds for each setting. Here is the plain-language version. Full numbers and caveats are in the repository.

Survival alone helped a little, not a lot.

Creatures selected only by surviving and having children scored about 9% higher on our energy-use measure than creatures whose parents were chosen at random. A real but small effect.

The world has room to do much better.

A forager we programmed by hand gets about half its energy from the big wells, and it finds them in every one of 30 ponds. Evolved creatures got about 2% of their energy there. So most of the available leftover energy went unused.

Our main prediction failed.

We predicted that scarce food, inheritance, and the real cost of dying would each be needed to reach the big wells. The opposite happened. Removing any of them made it easier to find them. Try it below.

Switch conditions on and off

Each dot is one of 30 ponds. A lit dot means a creature opened a big well at least once. Real results, not a simulation.

Caution: "opened at least once" is a generous test. It does not mean the creatures kept using the big wells. With everything switched on, they get only a few percent of their energy from them.

One idea we tested and could not confirm.

We guessed that opening a well is a favour to everyone nearby, so selection punishes the creature that pays for it. We tested that by letting the opener keep most of the reward. It moved in the predicted direction, but not by enough to be sure it was real at 30 ponds.

4 · What this does not show

Read this part.

  • It is a toy. Whether these ingredients are what mattered on Earth is an assumption.
  • The creatures' brains are tiny and shaped to follow gradients. A generic network did not learn useful behaviour in our budget, so "evolved" here means a small, well-shaped controller got tuned.
  • The energy accounting involves an assumption. We count a creature burning energy as producing more entropy than the same energy leaking away. That choice makes "capture it" look good by definition.
  • The design was adjusted after early failures. A world tuned until selection beats chance tends to show selection beating chance.
  • We are not claiming novelty. This is a test of an old idea, not a discovery of it.
