# Causal model: from stellar gradients to information-guided agency

**Status:** Working causal scaffold, version 0.1  
**Purpose:** Separate the transitions that the thermodynamic emergence hypothesis proposes to investigate.

## 1. Causal sequence

Stellar and planetary boundary conditions  
→ persistent energy and exergy gradients  
→ driven physical and chemical processes  
→ non-equilibrium structures and patterns  
→ self-maintaining organization  
→ replication, heredity and variation  
→ differential persistence and reproduction  
→ selection on information-use mechanisms  
→ sensing, memory, prediction and decisions  
→ action and environmental control (agency)  
→ possible evolution of flexible intelligence  
→ changes in planetary energy flows.

This is not a deterministic pipeline. Every arrow is a causal question with alternative outcomes, bottlenecks and feedbacks.

## 2. Stages, mechanisms and evidence requirements

| Stage | Proposed causal mechanism | Evidence required | Important alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boundary conditions → gradients | Stellar radiation, planetary heat, chemical reservoirs and environmental transport establish disequilibria | Explicit energy, exergy and entropy-flow accounting | Gradients may be weak, short-lived or inaccessible to relevant chemistry |
| Gradients → driven processes | Fluxes drive photochemistry, redox reactions, transport and other irreversible processes | Rate laws, flux measurements and boundary controls | Ordinary relaxation or passive forcing may explain observed patterns |
| Driven processes → organization | Nonlinear kinetics, feedback, catalysis, reaction-diffusion and self-assembly produce patterns | Formation from non-organized initial conditions; comparison with passive controls | Transient pattern formation without functional persistence |
| Organization → self-maintenance | Coupled processes use flows to regenerate components, boundaries or catalytic activity | Perturbation, resource-interruption and recovery experiments; maintenance budget | Persistence from stored resources or external control |
| Self-maintenance → replication | A system's chemistry or organization produces additional systems or copies | Specified replication mechanism, material and energy budget | Copying machinery or template supplied by the modeller |
| Replication → evolution | Heritable variation plus differential reproductive success changes lineage composition | Lineage continuity, fidelity, mutation and selection measurements | Replication without cumulative heredity |
| Evolution → information use | Information improves decisions about resources or threats enough to offset sensing, storage and computation costs | Matched information/no-information tests and cost sweeps | Fixed heuristics or passive traits perform as well |
| Information use → agency | Actions causally change future states in ways selected policies exploit | Intervention tests, counterfactual action controls and control-theoretic measures | Behaviour is reactive, hard-coded or has no causal effect |
| Agency → intelligence | Flexible information-guided control generalizes across tasks, uncertainty and changing environments | Transfer, robustness, learning and multi-task measures | Narrow specialization without general capability |
| Organization → planetary effects | Structures change absorption, reflection, chemical storage, transport and heat/radiation export | Matched planetary budgets with all relevant pathways | Effects cancel, reverse sign or redistribute dissipation without increasing total |

## 3. The thermodynamic environment is not one undifferentiated gradient

The star–planet system supplies a large-scale energy context, but abiogenesis and biological maintenance occur in local environments. A global planetary energy budget cannot substitute for local chemical thermodynamics.

Relevant local disequilibria may include:

- Photochemical gradients and wavelength-dependent photon flux.
- Redox couples and electron donors/acceptors.
- Proton, ion and pH gradients.
- Temperature gradients and phase boundaries.
- Chemical-potential gradients across compartments or mineral surfaces.
- Wet–dry, freeze–thaw and other cyclic environmental forcing.

A useful model must identify which gradient drives which reaction or process, the rate at which work can be extracted, and the material constraints that limit it.

## 4. Feedbacks

The forward chain is supplemented by feedbacks:

- Organization can alter local gradients through absorption, catalysis, transport, insulation, concentration or depletion.
- Self-maintaining systems can modify their own boundary conditions.
- Replication changes the abundance and spatial distribution of structures, which changes competition and shared environmental effects.
- Information-guided action can alter resource access and future observations.
- Biospheres can alter planetary albedo, atmospheric composition, hydrology and chemical cycling. The sign of these effects is environment-dependent.

Feedbacks must be represented as physical mechanisms, not generic positive multipliers.

## 5. Nested system boundaries

Use nested control volumes rather than one vague "universe" boundary:

1. **Radiative source–receiver:** star, orbit and planetary top-of-atmosphere boundary.
2. **Planet:** atmosphere, surface, oceans, subsurface and biosphere.
3. **Local chemical environment:** specified fluid, mineral, pore, surface or compartment.
4. **Dissipative structure:** the pattern or network and its immediate exchanges.
5. **Replicator / lineage:** physical maintenance and replication costs, material flows and inherited states.
6. **Agent / population:** information acquisition, computation, action, survival and reproduction.

For each boundary, report energy and entropy storage, transfers in and out, and internal entropy generation. Exergy requires a declared reference environment. Do not add internal transfers twice when aggregating nested budgets.

## 6. Causal identification principles

- Compare driven and undriven systems.
- Compare self-maintaining and passive structures.
- Distinguish spontaneous formation from imposed initial organization.
- Distinguish replication from heredity and evolution.
- Compare information-guided policies against matched reactive, random and passive baselines.
- Measure the physical costs of sensing, memory, computation, movement and actuation.
- Measure planetary entropy production independently of fitness and energy throughput.
- Test alternative mechanisms and boundary definitions.
- Allow null outcomes at every transition.

## 7. The key conceptual distinction

The hypothesis does not say that entropy increase directly causes intelligence. It proposes a chain of physically mediated transitions in which energy and chemical disequilibria make some processes possible, organization changes persistence, replication enables evolution, and selection can favour information-guided control in environments where it pays.

The causal work lies in explaining each transition and its constraints.
